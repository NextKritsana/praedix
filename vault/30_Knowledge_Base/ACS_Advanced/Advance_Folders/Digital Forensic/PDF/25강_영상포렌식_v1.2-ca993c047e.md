Codec
Codec is short for "Compressor-decompressor" and refers to a technology or device that
compresses or decompresses digital video or audio data. The purpose of a codec is to
reduce the size of a file in order to store and transmit data efficiently, preserving the
quality of digital media, saving storage space, and enabling faster transmission over the
internet.
➢ Video codecs: H.264, H.265 (HEVC), VP9, AV1, and others, which are responsible for
compressing the video stream.
➢ Audio codecs: Responsible for compressing audio streams, such as AAC, MP3, Opus,
Vorbis, etc. Codecs are selected based on compression efficiency, compatibility,
license terms, etc.
Container
A container is a file format that can contain one or more video and audio streams,
possibly subtitles, menus, metadata, and more. The container acts as a "box" to hold
data compressed with various codecs, and the file's extension (.mp4, .avi, .mkv, etc.)
refers to the commonly used container format.

What is H.264?
H.264, officially known as MPEG-4 Part 10 or Advanced Video Coding (AVC), is a standard for video
compression.
It was jointly developed in 2003 by the ITU-T Video Coding Experts Group and the ISO/IEC Moving Picture
Experts Group. H.264 provides highly efficient video compression, allowing the same video quality to be
transmitted at a lower bitrate than previous standards, which can significantly reduce network bandwidth
usage, and is widely used in a variety of applications, including digital broadcasting, internet streaming,
endpoint surveillance cameras, video communications, and more.
Key Features
Efficient video compression: Allows high-quality video to be transmitted at a lower bitrate.
Flexible: Designed for a variety of network environments and devices
Error resilience: Robust handling of errors that may occur during video transmission over the network
Support for multiple frame types: Efficient compression and quality control, including I-frames, P-frames, B-
frames, and more.

   Network Abstraction Layer
Network Abstraction Layer (NAL) is one of the core components of the H.264/AVC
(Advanced Video Coding) standard, designed for the efficient transmission of video data. Its main
purpose is to package video content for a network or storage medium
Key features of NAL
1.Data abstraction: Abstracts video data generated by the Video Coding Layer (VCL) into a form
suitable for a network or storage medium
2.Efficient data transfer and storage: Video data is packed into NAL Units (NALUs) for transmission.
Each NALU consists of a header and payload, where the header indicates the type of NALU, the
importance of the data, etc.
3.Error robustness: Designed to be robust to errors that can occur when transmitting video over a
network
4.Variable quality of service (QoS): Enables you to adjust the quality of service (QoS) of video
streams to meet different network environments and requirements.
NAL Unit Type - NALs are categorized into several types
Sequence Parameter Set (SPS): Defines the overall characteristics of a video sequence
Picture Parameter Set (PPS): defines the coding characteristics of each frame (or field)
Instantaneous Decoder Refresh (IDR) frame: A key frame that allows the decoder to start decoding
the video from this frame.
Coded Slice: NALU containing actual video data
* NAL = NAL Unit + Payloads,NAL Unit = Start prefix + NAL TYPE

NAL Units
Start Prefix: A bit pattern indicating the start of a NAL unit, typically in the form of
0x000001 or 0x00000001, which is used to unambiguously identify the start of a NAL
unit within a video stream.
NAL TYPE: A field indicating the type of NAL unit, which distinguishes the type of video
data (e.g., SPS, PPS, IDR frames, etc.). The NAL type determines what role the NAL unit
plays.
Relationship between SPS, PPS, and IDR frames
SPS and PPS provide the overall information and detailed settings needed to decode a
video stream, and IDR frames lay the groundwork for decoding to begin at specific points
in the video based on these settings. Therefore, correctly identifying and interpreting SPS
and PPS within the video stream is essential for accurate decoding and playback of the
video.

Slice
Slice, divides a video frame into multiple parts, each of which can be decoded independently. This
structure contributes to improving the efficiency and error robustness of the video stream.
Key features of slices
1.Independent decoding: Each slice can be decoded independently, so if some data is lost during
transmission, the rest of the video stream is not affected, which improves error robustness, especially in
networked environments.
2.Parallelism: The slice-based structure enables parallel processing, which can increase decoding
efficiency, as it can utilize multicore processors to process multiple slices simultaneously.
3.Flexible bitrate Slices allow you to dynamically adjust the bitrate of a video stream, allowing you to
flexibly adjust video quality to maintain optimal delivery when network conditions change.
Slice type
H.264 defines different slice types to achieve optimal coding efficiency based on the characteristics of the
video content.
I slice (Intra-coded Slice): This slice contains image data that can be decoded independently, without
reference to data from other frames. Primarily used when there is a major scene change.
P-slice (Predictive-coded Slice): Containing predictively coded data by referencing data from previous
frames, P-slices can efficiently represent small changes within the same scene of a video.
B-slice (Bi-predictive-coded Slice): Contains predictively coded data with references to both previous and
subsequent frames. B-slices provide more efficient data compression than P-slices, but the decoding
process is more complex.

Macroblock Macroblocks (MB) are used as the basic unit of video coding and compression. They divide video frames into
smaller blocks and process these blocks independently to increase the storage and transmission efficiency of video
data.
Organization of Macro Blocks
Macro blocks are typically 16x16 pixels in size. This size contains a luma channel, or brightness information, and
two chroma channels, or color information. Chroma sampling is usually done using a 4:2:0 format, so that the
chroma channels per macro block are represented by an 8x8 pixel size.
Functionality of macro blocks
Efficient video compression: Macro blocks efficiently compress data by eliminating spatial and temporal
redundancy within video frames.
Predictive coding: H.264 uses two types of frames: I-frames (intra-coding frames) and P/B-frames (inter-coding
frames). Macroblocks achieve efficient compression by performing intra- or inter-prediction within these frames.
Intra prediction uses data from other macro blocks within the same frame, while inter prediction references data
from earlier or later frames.
Motion estimation and compensation: In intercoding frames, motion estimation and compensation techniques are
crucial: the macroblock identifies changes in the current frame relative to the previous frame in a video sequence
with motion, and uses them to maximize compression efficiency by coding only changes in the video data..
Error resilience: By processing video data in macroblocks, even if some data is lost during transmission, the rest of
the video can still be decoded normally without affecting the rest of the video. This improves the resiliency of the
video stream in the event of errors during network transmission.

Parameter sets Parameter Sets are a critical component for efficient transmission and decoding of video
streams.Parameter Sets contain information that defines the composition and characteristics of a video
stream, They are primarily categorized into Sequence Parameter Sets (SPS) and Picture Parameter Sets
(PPS).
Sequence Parameter Set (SPS)
Sequence parameter sets define the overall characteristics of a video sequence.
Profile and Level: Define the capabilities and performance levels of the codec.
Chroma Format: Specifies how color difference is sampled.
Resolution and frame rate: Defines the width, height, and number of frames per second of the video.
Entropy Coding Mode: Specifies the entropy coding method, such as Context-based Adaptive Binary
Arithmetic Coding (CABAC) or Context-based Adaptive Variable Length Coding (CAVLC).
Temporal and spatial video structure: Describes the relationships between frames and the structural
characteristics of the video.
Picture Parameter Set (PPS)
The picture parameter set defines the coding characteristics for each frame (or field)
Slice groups: Defines how video frames are divided into different slice groups
Weighted Prediction: Specifies the weighting parameters used for interframe prediction.
Entropy coding settings: Define detailed settings for the entropy coding method selected by SPS
Deblocking filter settings: Specify filter settings to reduce blockiness in images

If the NAL unit type says SPS PPS, why is the parameter set also SPS PPS?
Why are parameter sets included in NAL units?
1.Accessibility and compatibility: By packaging the SPS and PPS in NAL units and embedding them
within the stream, the decoder can easily find all the information it needs when it starts to
interpret the stream. This is especially useful when starting to decode from the middle of the
stream
2.Efficient stream management: When video streams are transmitted over a variety of network
environments, NAL units (e.g., SPS, PPS) containing sensitive information may be specially
managed to maintain the quality and compatibility of the can be specifically managed to maintain
the quality and compatibility of the stream.
3.Error resilience: By separating the SPS and PPS on a per NAL basis, error resilience is improved,
allowing other parts of the stream to be played even if this information is corrupted or lost. SPS
and PPS can be retransmitted if necessary, which increases the reliability of the video stream.

What is AAC?
AAC (Advanced Audio Coding) is a standard for compressing audio files and is
considered the successor to MP3, providing better audio quality at the same bitrate.
It was introduced in 1997 with MPEG-2 and further developed in MPEG-4.AAC is
widely used in high-definition audio streaming, digital broadcasting, and a variety of
digital audio applications.
Key Features
Improved audio quality: Better audio quality than MP3 at lower bitrates
Multichannel audio support: Supports multi-channel audio formats, including 5.1-
channel surround sound as well as stereo.
Efficient compression algorithms: Efficiently compresses audio data to reduce file size
while maintaining high audio quality.
Broad compatibility: Used by a variety of digital audio players, smartphones, computer
systems, and broadcast.

Audio Data Interchange Format (ADIF) is one of the formats for storing Advanced Audio Coding
(AAC) audio streams in files. ADIF is primarily used for file-based storage and transfer, not for streaming. The
format organizes bitstreams of AAC audio data into one contiguous block, At the beginning of the file is a
header that contains general information needed to decode the audio stream.

MP4 container
A general-purpose digital multimedia container format that can contain video and audio streams, as well as other data
types (e.g., subtitles, menus). It is optimized for efficient streaming and distribution over the internet, and is often used
for high-definition video.
Advanced video compression: Supports advanced video coding standards such as H.264/AVC orH.265/HEVC

Interactivity: Supports configuration of user interface elements and menus to create interactive multimedia applications

capabilities of MP4 as a container for audio content. The format is especially used for music files, and offers high audio
quality and low file size.
Efficient audio compression: Primarily using the Advanced Audio Coding (AAC) audio coding format, provides better

Lossy compression: Reduces the size of audio data while maintaining high audio quality.
