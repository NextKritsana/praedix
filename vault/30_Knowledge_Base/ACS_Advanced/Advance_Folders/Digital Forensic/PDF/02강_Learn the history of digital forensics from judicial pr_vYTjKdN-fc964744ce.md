The concept and meaning of digital evidence01
What is digital evidence?
⚫ Any digital data that can prove a crime or provide a link between a crime and a victim or offender
⚫ Data stored or transmitted using a computer as information that can prove or refute how a crime occurred,
or that can lead to key elements of a crime, such as criminal intent or an alibi (W. Jerry Chisum)
⚫ Digital information, such as evidentially valuable information stored or transmitted in digital form(SWGDE)
⚫ Information stored or transmitted in binary form (IOCE) that can be trusted in court
⚫ Digital information that is stored on various digital storage media or transmitted on network equipment and
wired or wireless communications that has value as evidence because its authenticity can be guaranteed
As shown above, the lack of an established definition has led to similar terms being used interchangeably

The principle that evidence obtained by illegal means, or evidence
derived therefrom, should be excluded from the court
The Fourth Amendment protects Americans' presumption of
innocence and against unfair investigations
The fruit tree theory of evidence is closely related to the "illegally
obtained evidence exclusionary rule," which states the principle that
evidence that is illegally obtained and any evidence discovered or
derived from it should be excluded from court
In other words, the phrase "fruit of the tree" implies that the
evidence was obtained through illegal behavior, i.e., the evidence
was obtained unfairly

Silverthorne Lumber Co. v. United States, 1920 In this case, the
government obtained corporate books and documents of
Silverthorne Lumber Co. through an illegal search.
The information is inadmissible in court.
The principle was applied that both the original evidence obtained
illegally (the poisonous tree) and the additional evidence obtained
as a result (the fruit of that tree) were not admissible.
This is because the confession obtained through the illegal method
of torture was ' the poisonous tree' and the company books and
documents discovered as a result were ' the fruit of that tree’
