Consume large amounts of time and resources
• Brute force attacks are very simple, but they are also very time and resource intensive
• The time required for an attack increases exponentially with the length and complexity of the
password
• This is called "key space" and is determined by the number of characters available
and the length of the password
Brute Force Thresholds
Exam
If your password uses only lowercase alphabets and is 6 characters long, the keyspace is 26^6 (about 30 million)
If the password is 8 characters long, including upper and lower case alphabets and numbers, the keyspace is 62^8 (about 200 billion)
Therefore, the effectiveness of a brute force attack is highly dependent on the security policy of the target system and the complexity of the
password

Account lockout policies
Microsoft Windows provides a protection called the account lockout policy
This is the ability to temporarily lock an account if a user enters an incorrect password more than a certain number of times
Default threshold set to 10
If a user enters an incorrect password 10 times in a row, their account is locked for a period of time
During this time, you won't be able to sign in to your account, even if you enter the correct password
Default threshold
• To protect user accounts from security threats such as brute force attacks
• If the account lockout policy is enabled, hackers will be locked out of their accounts after a certain
number of incorrect password attempts, providing some protection against these attacks
Why?
• Account lockout policies don't always provide complete protection
• If a hacker knows the username of a target account, he or she can target this account and deliberately
lock the account by repeatedly entering the wrong password (account lockout attack)
Caveats
